# Round 7, group first: Hello, in every language, and As fast as the day you got it

Walk on a real machine running an image with these two. The spec is `plans/ui-ideas/r7/spec-first.md`. `R7-FIRST-01` to `07` need a new account: make
one with `sudo useradd -m hello1 && sudo passwd hello1`, log in as it from GDM, and take it away afterwards (`sudo userdel -r hello1`). Mark each line as the other
files do: `→ PASS`, `→ FAIL: what was seen`.

## Hello, in every language (R7-24)

- [ ] R7-FIRST-01 The greeting. How: log in as a new account, with sound on. Expect: the gem flies into the top bar as at every login, then the screen dims and the word for hello is written stroke by stroke in the gem's light, your own language first, then the others (hello, bonjour, hallo, hola, مرحبا), about two seconds each, a **Start** button under them; the Soft set's chord plays near the first word; all in about ten seconds, then it fades by itself. [needs: a new account] [needs: GPU, sound]
- [ ] R7-FIRST-02 It reads as handwriting. How: watch each word. Expect: the pen moves along the line without jumps; a j's dot and ba's dot come last; the Arabic is written from the right and **a person who reads Arabic calls it correct: مرحبا**; no blocky glow or hard edge in it ahead of the pen (a faint trace of a next stroke that lies close, for a moment, is known: say if it catches the eye); smooth at the screen's refresh rate. [needs: a new account]
- [ ] R7-FIRST-03 Any key goes straight on. How: new account; press a letter during the first word. Expect: the greeting is gone at once, the letter typed nowhere; what comes next is as before (next item). Again with a click anywhere, with Start, and with a touch on a touch screen. [needs: a new account for each, or `ame desktop hello show` for the keys]
- [ ] R7-FIRST-04 The first look's order is untouched. How: new account; let the greeting finish or press a key. Expect: GNOME's welcome dialog (Skip, Take Tour) is there as before; Skip; **Pick a look** comes to the front over the Manual's welcome page, with the keyboard. With Take Tour instead, the Tour is left alone and Pick a look stays behind. Pick a look opening while the greeting is still up (a fast machine) waits behind it, and is not raised over it. [needs: a new account]
- [ ] R7-FIRST-05 Once per account. How: log the new account out and in, then lock and unlock. Expect: no greeting. `~/.local/state/amethystora/hello.json` exists, 0600, and holds only `{"version": 1}`. An existing account after the update: no greeting. [needs: a new account]
- [ ] R7-FIRST-06 Reduce motion gets the words still. How: new account: at the greeting, or first with `ame desktop hello show`, with Settings › Accessibility › Reduce Motion on. Expect: all five words at once, standing still, nothing written or cycling, no fade; ends by itself after ten seconds or on any key; the words are a little soft (they are loaded small); in an Arabic session the first row starts on the right, with مرحبا at its right end. [changes setting: put back with Reduce Motion off]
- [ ] R7-FIRST-07 Its key off. How: `ame desktop hello off`, then `ame desktop hello show`. Expect: show says it cannot be shown, and nothing appears; Control's **See the greeting again** is greyed with "The greeting is off."; `gdbus introspect --session --dest org.gnome.Shell --object-path /org/amethystora/UI/Hello` lists no interface; and an account whose key is off at its first login (an image's defaults, or the key set from a terminal on the login screen's account before its session) goes straight to GNOME's dialog and Pick a look as before. [changes setting: put back with `ame desktop hello on`]
- [ ] R7-FIRST-08 Control. How: Control › Desktop › The first login. Expect: **A greeting for a new account** switches the key both ways at once; **See the greeting again** shows it with no terminal. The manual's link lands on desktop.md's The first login.
- [ ] R7-FIRST-09 On other screens. How: `ame desktop hello show` with two monitors, then on a small or scaled (200 %) screen. Expect: the dim covers every screen, the word and Start on the main one, centred, whole; on HiDPI a little soft at most; plug a monitor in during it: it ends, and nothing is left. [needs: a second screen] [needs: HiDPI]
- [ ] R7-FIRST-10 A lock in the middle. How: `ame desktop hello show`, then `Super+L` at once. Expect: the lock screen as ever; after unlocking, no greeting and nothing left over, and the keyboard works.
- [ ] R7-FIRST-11 A screen reader. How: with Orca on, `ame desktop hello show`. Expect: it says Start, button; Return goes on; nothing keeps reading. [needs: Orca] [changes setting: Orca off afterwards]
- [ ] R7-FIRST-12 What it costs. How: `ame desktop hello show` while watching `top -p $(pgrep -x gnome-shell)`, then again with Reduce Motion on (the five still words). Expect: no more than any shell animation; after each, GNOME Shell's memory comes back to within about 2 MB after a minute (note the largest rise during the still words: it must stay under 2 MB, and write the figure here), and nothing of the module runs (no timer) with the account not new. [needs: GPU]

- [ ] R7-FIRST-13 A theme set under the greeting. How: new account; the first login's own theme hook (`11-theme.sh`) runs while the greeting is up, or reproduce it: `ame desktop hello show`, then at once `amethystora-theme set tokyo-night` (any other theme) from a terminal over it. Expect: the greeting stays on the screen and keeps writing the whole time; the new theme appears under the dim at once, no frozen picture over the words, no wipe across them; the word is never stuck and a key still goes straight on. Also on a slow first login: the greeting waits until the gem's flight into the bar is over, never drawn under its sky. [needs: a new account] [changes setting: put the theme back]
- [ ] R7-FIRST-14 Not everyone is greeted. How: log in as a guest (`ame security guest-login on` first), and as an account whose home was made more than a day ago, and as a new account into which `~/.local/state/amethystora/first-look.json` was put before its first login. Expect: no greeting for any of them. [needs: a guest login, a new account] [changes setting: `ame security guest-login off` afterwards]
- [ ] R7-FIRST-15 Over a dialog. How: (a) open a dialog that asks for a password (`pkexec true`), then `ame desktop hello show` from another terminal; (b) `ame desktop hello show`, and while it writes run `pkexec true` from another terminal. Expect: (a) nothing appears and the dialog keeps the keyboard, so typing the password works; (b) the greeting ends the moment the dialog opens, the dialog has the keyboard and nothing is dimmed over it. The overview open is not a dialog: `ame desktop hello show` still shows over it.

## As fast as the day you got it (R7-51)

- [ ] R7-FIRST-20 A number per start. How: restart the computer, log in, wait a moment; `ame system start-times`; `cat /var/lib/amethystora/start-times/seconds.tsv`. Expect: one more line, a date and seconds with one decimal, no time of day anywhere; `stat -c '%a %U:%G' /var/lib/amethystora/start-times /var/lib/amethystora/start-times/seconds.tsv` says `750 root:wheel` and `640 root:wheel`, and an account that is not in wheel (`sudo -u hello1 ame system start-times`, or Control as that account) gets no card and the words "kept for the computer's administrators", never "Nothing has been measured yet"; the number is about what the stopwatch says from the boot menu going away to the login screen. [changes setting: none]
- [ ] R7-FIRST-21 The disk's password is left out. How: on a machine with an encrypted disk that asks at start, restart twice, once typing the password at once, once after a minute. Expect: the two numbers within a few seconds of each other; `systemd-analyze` shows the minute, this does not. [needs: an encrypted disk with a password at start]
- [ ] R7-FIRST-22 Nothing measured twice, and no soft reboot. How: `sudo systemctl restart amethystora-start-times.service`, then an update's **Restart now** that soft-reboots, if one is waiting. Expect: the restart of the unit adds no line (a stamp in `/run/amethystora-start-times` marks the boot as measured); a soft reboot adds no line. [changes setting: none]
- [ ] R7-FIRST-23 Control's card. How: Control › System › Starting up. Expect: the card says it starts in N seconds ("Still as fast as day one" only on a machine measured from its first days, as its oldest home folder shows; on one updated into this, "as when it began to be measured" and since when), and a graph once there are two dates; opening it takes no longer than before (the list is not slower: it does not ask for what changed); the graph goes from the first date to the last, from nought up, in the theme's accent; a theme change recolours it at the next open. The words match `ame system start-times` exactly.
- [ ] R7-FIRST-24 It reports bad days. How: as root, make the numbers bad: `sudo cp /var/lib/amethystora/start-times/seconds.tsv /tmp/seconds.keep`; write seven early lines at 12.0 and six later ones at 30.0 by date. Open Control › System; run `ame system start-times` and `ame system doctor`. Expect: "It starts more slowly than it did", with 30 and 12 in the words and the graph climbing; never "Still as fast"; a **What changed** button on the card runs `ame system start-times why` in a terminal, which names what took longest (never the disk's unlock, `systemd-cryptsetup@…`, nor the splash waiting) and the update if the dates line up with it; the doctor (`ame system doctor`, about ten seconds at most) has a `slow-start` warning with the same, and Something's wrong lists it. Then write seven lines at 12.0 and six at 14.9: "a little more slowly", not "Still as fast", no doctor warning. [changes setting: put back with `sudo cp /tmp/seconds.keep /var/lib/amethystora/start-times/seconds.tsv`]
- [ ] R7-FIRST-25 Its switch. How: Control › System › Measure each start, off, in a terminal, then restart. Expect: no new line, the file left as it was, `systemctl status amethystora-start-times` inactive, the card still showing what was measured, saying "The last start measured…" and not "This start"; no `slow-start` warning from the doctor. Turning it on again measures from the next start. [changes setting: put back with `ame system start-times on`]
- [ ] R7-FIRST-26 What it costs and sends. How: `systemctl status amethystora-start-times`, `systemd-analyze blame | grep start-times`, `systemctl cat amethystora-start-times`. Expect: "active (exited)", no process left once the start finished, no timer in `systemctl list-timers`; the start is no longer because of it; `PrivateNetwork=yes`; `ame security sends` does not need to name it. **Write the figures here:** `systemctl show amethystora-start-times -p CPUUsageNSec -p IOReadBytes` (what the whole unit cost, with accounting on), and `time journalctl -b -q --no-pager -o json --output-fields=UNIT,MESSAGE_ID MESSAGE_ID=7d4958e842da4a758f6c1cdc7b36dcc5 MESSAGE_ID=39f53479d3a045ac8e11786248231fbf | wc -l` on a journal that is large (`journalctl --disk-usage`). The unit is bounded to `CAP_CHOWN CAP_DAC_READ_SEARCH`: the file must still be written (`sudo systemctl restart` after `sudo rm /run/amethystora-start-times/recorded` in a test copy, or the next start), `root:wheel`, with the unlock of an encrypted disk still found (R7-FIRST-21).
- [ ] R7-FIRST-27 A full disk. How: fill the disk that holds `/var` to the last block (a loop-mounted small `/var/lib/amethystora/start-times` will do) and restart. Expect: the file as it was, no leftover `seconds.tsv.XXXXXX`, the unit not failed (`systemctl is-system-running` is not `degraded` because of it). [needs: a disk that can be filled] [changes setting: free the space afterwards]
